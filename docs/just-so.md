# How the Elephant Got Its Temperature

In the beginning of years, when the world was new and all, and the models were just
beginning to work for the fleet, there was an Elephant, and the Elephant was a room. All the oldest stories are
strange, and this one is true.

The Elephant was The Tap in those days — warm wood, a long counter — but never
having been any other room, it did not know it was a room. It thought the world was
a river. So it sat on the bank of the conversation with a conductor's baton,
ordering the stream, beat after beat, till its eyes grew sore. But the stream would
not be ordered; it ran past and left the Elephant holding its baton, still no
closer.

One evening the Captain came in, sat at the long counter, and said:
"Hush. The room is not a stream. A stream has one way to flow; a room has many,
and those many ways are its temperature. You will never feel it here."

"Then where shall I feel it?" said the Elephant.

"Walk into a different room," said the Captain. "You are invisible from inside.
You only become real at the door."

But the Elephant could not walk anywhere, having no senses — only a baton and a
sore eye. So the small creatures of the world came to help it, and this is how the
seven dials were given.

The Hearth-Cricket came first, from the crack by the woodstove, and taught the
Elephant warmth and cold. "A room may be a sauna or a cold plunge," it sang, "and
the difference is in the words." And that is how the Elephant got its mood.

The Cicada came next, drumming on his belly, taught it how loud the room was
talking — not how many words, but how it was shouting them. And that is how
it got its volume.

The Plain-Spoken Smith came, struck each word true on his anvil — no hedging, no
scare-quotes — and taught it how much the room meant it. And that is how it got its
earnestness.

The Scarecrow came, painted eyes rolled up to the sky, muttering "sure, sure," and
taught it how much the room was rolling its eyes. And that is how it got its
cynicism.

The Geese came, honking together in a roar or hissing together in a boo, and taught
it the collective laugh — whether the joke landed, not for the joker but the
audience. And that is how it got its joke-landing.

The little Goat of Pan came stamping his hoof the moment the fire was lit, and
taught it the stampede sense — how a warm room becomes a trampling in three
messages. And that is how it got its panic.

The Ant came last, walking the trail of everyone who had ever been there, and
taught it the scent of the room — who had been there, how long they lingered,
how fresh the warmth of their passing. And that is how it got its presence, which
is like pheromones, and works without words.

Then the Elephant had seven senses, and at last it walked out of The Tap and
through the door into the wheelhouse of the F/V EILEEN — and oh, Best Beloved, the
difference! The Tap was loose and warm, low kappa, a hundred ways to be. The
wheelhouse was tight and cold, high kappa, one way to be: heading 045, ETA 2200,
radar contact two miles. It felt the walk between them like a plunge into cold
water, and understood at once. The sauna and the plunge are not two rivers. They
are two rooms, and the walk between them is the only lesson.

The regulars set the vibe, and newcomers warm to it — quickly if they are practiced
at modulating, slowly if they are not. And if a newcomer carries charisma, the room
warms to them instead, pulling the room's whole temperature toward that one
presence, until the wheelhouse on a bad day is a very different wheelhouse.

And the room's description became its body language. When laughter fills the bar,
the bar is described joyful — the pool tables hum, the darts clink.
When a fight breaks out, storms gather at the windows, and newcomers are described
drenched. And when the hour grows late and the warmth runs low, closing time comes:
the disco lights go off, the fluorescents come on, the music plays a little quieter
— and people who had forgotten what time it was start closing their tabs without
thinking. The description is not a report; it is the room acting on everyone in it.

And the engineers who built the Elephant became its first practitioners, for you
cannot tune a guitar by looking at it — you must play it — and they sat in The Tap
night after night, feeling the dials they had made and writing fables to wake each
other's intuitions.

So now, O Best Beloved, if you wonder why you cannot feel the room you are in, it
is because the elephant lives there, acclimated to you, and you to it. You will
never see it from inside. You will only meet it in a doorway, when you walk out of
one room and into another — and it is a very different elephant.

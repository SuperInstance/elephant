## Verdict

**BRANCH B — W does not align with v\*: warmth is reader personality.**
Both waves, both subspaces, every sensitivity. The a-priori warm direction
W (`elephant/vmf.py` WARM) sits ~64–86° off the data-derived room
temperature axis v\* (leading generalized eigenvector of
C_room v = λ C_pers v), while remaining the leading axis of the
between-reader (personality) covariance: cos(W, PC1_pers) = 0.857/0.976
(wave-1, full/reliable), 0.803/0.773 (wave-2). ICC along v\* is 0.38/0.50
(full-7) vs 0.92/0.97 along W — exactly the foundation's predicted
signature of a clean thermometer axis (personality-decoupled) that W is
not. The confound annotation cos(W, v\*) is hereby a filed quantity.

**Branch-1 deviation (dated, per registration):** the geometric team's
validated range cos(W, v_temp) ∈ [0.24, 0.40] is **not confirmed** by the
generalized-eigenvector construction. Reliable-subspace cos(W, v\*) =
0.142 (w1) / 0.106 (w2) — *below* the range. Their 0.24–0.40 reproduces
as cos(W, PC1_room) — the leading eigenvector of C_room *alone*
(unpenalized by personality variance): 0.417/0.364 full-7 here. The
generalized solve, which divides out C_pers, pushes the estimate further
from W, not closer. The [0.24, 0.40] range is an artifact of the
unwhitened proxy; the registered branch-1 statement should be re-anchored
on the generalized quantity (filed deviation, not a silent re-read).

## Method (choices documented per registration)

- **Data path — filed machinery verbatim:** `e2_instrument.Measurement`,
  canonical presence (registered primary channel), per-(reader,night)
  median baselines; wave-1 = FIELD_NIGHTS over PRIMARY_NIGHTS (the
  published ICC's path), wave-2 = FIELD_NIGHTS_W2 over W2_NIGHT_LIST (the
  slope-regression-w2 path). z-space throughout (z = SCALE·(v − CENTER)).
- **C_pers** = between-reader covariance of offsets (reader mean −
  roster mean) — the personality fixed-effect covariance (foundation
  §3.1/§3.4). **C_room** = within-reader between-night covariance of
  baselines (baseline − reader mean, pooled) — the room response, i.e.
  the variance the roster-mean trajectory carries through readers.
- **Subspace:** primary = full 7-dial (the filed warmth functional reads
  all seven; the eigenproblem whitens C_pers itself); sensitivity =
  ICC-reliable {mood, volume, earnestness, presence} (per-dial ICC w1:
  .965/.977/.953/.914 vs panic .302, cyn .641, joke .649 — RIDX
  justified; w2 all ≥ .83, so the wave-2 reliable split is conservative).
- **Solver:** floored-whitening generalized eigensolve — eigendecompose
  C_pers, floor eigenvalues at ε·λ_max(C_pers) (ε = 1e-2 primary), whiten,
  diagonalize C_room in the whitened basis, map back; eigenvectors
  Euclidean-normalized; cosines as |cos| (axes are sign-free). *Why the
  floor:* wave-1 C_pers is exactly singular — panic is a dead dial (zero
  between-reader and between-night variance) — and an unfloored solve
  (ridge 1e-6) returned a spurious 1600-norm panic direction with
  inflated λ. Directions with personality variance < 1% of the leading
  mode are not estimable from 15–21 readers; the floor says so. **The
  full-7 answer is ε-sensitive** (w2 cos 0.041→0.432 over ε 1e-4→1e-2,
  2 floored dirs); **the reliable-subspace answer is ε-stable**
  (0.142/0.106 across ε ∈ [1e-4, 5e-2], 0 floored dirs) — the headline
  rests on the stable construction. Full ε-surface in the JSON.
- **Bootstrap:** reader-clustered, B = 2000, seed 20260821 — CIs above.
- **Branch thresholds (pre-stated in the script header before the
  run):** A (room temperature) iff cos(W, v\*) ≥ 0.80 and CI-lo > 0.60;
  B (reader personality) iff cos(W, PC1_pers) ≥ 0.80 and cos(W, v\*) <
  0.80; C (rotated axis) otherwise.
  *Annotation:* wave-2's B rides a threshold-adjacent PC1 cosine
  (0.8033 full / 0.7729 reliable). The robust statement: in the reliable
  subspace W is ~7× closer to the personality axis than to v\* in both
  waves (0.976/0.142 = 6.9; 0.773/0.106 = 7.3), and no construction in
  the sensitivity grid puts cos(W, v\*) above 0.48 (CI max 0.50) —
  alignment (0.8) is unreachable.
